Glass matrix



           A PbWO4 crystal was heated up to 800◦ C. After
           the test, this crystal showed unaltered scintillating
           properties
           A sample was prepared using lead glass and a
           fraction equal to 30%w of PbWO4 fragments.
           The thermal cycle included:
                   A 20 minutes heating ramp from room temperature to
                   800-850◦ C
                   A 30 minutes long temperature dwell
                   Slow cooling down to room temperature (2 hours)
           The sample presented optical defects (air
           bubbles), but shows a correct inclusion of the SC
           fragments.

Conclusions



              The DIANA project aims to the realization of novel scintillators based on scintillating crystals in an
              amorphous medium, reaching 3 − 4 g·cm−3 densities
              The feasibility of DIANA is shown by the preliminary tests
              This novel material is easily workable and economically competitive
              This material can be employed in high energy physics experiments and as veto system (for example in
              rare-events physics).
              The synergies with the EEE collaboration and the Gießen University are already granted by supportive
              letters to the DIANA project
              The SC procurement at a competitive price is granted by the collaboration with Hilger Crystals.
              The risk analysis enlights the possibility to adapt the project even in the worst-case scenario.
