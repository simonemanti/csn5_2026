 Mineral detection of astroparticles: the base idea
 One of the many ways Geophysics has been connected to astroparticle physics over the years
        Based on a very simple energy deposit idea: Scattering + Track formation
                                An energetic astroparticle - WIMP, neutrino, Muons from
                                cosmic ray shower - hits an atom in the crystal lattice of a
                                mineral. The atom recoils and damages the crystalline
                                structure leaving a defect called ion track -or just track
Same formation concept as fission tracks - in that case the recoiling particle is the fission product
                                    The idea of looking for tracks caused by cosmic rays or more
                                    exotic particles has been floating around for 50 years
                                                                    with interest from scientists such as Occhialini




 Set aside because of limited modeling, readout and computational capabilities                                   2

   The concept was revived at the end of the 2010s and got the name paleodetectors

                              integrate exotic particle interactions over geological
                              timescales, offering a high exposure detection scenario.

                                  PRI𝝻S Summary
Objective: An innovative detection technique using optical microscopy to identify
cosmic ray-induced ion tracks in ancient minerals.
Key Milestones:

 ●   Gold: Establish track-length spectrum standard for paleodetector research.
 ●   Platinum: First confirmed identification of astroparticle-induced tracks.
 ●   Diamond: Reconstruct historical cosmic ray flux variations from geological samples.
Methods and Tools:

 ●   Sample collection from ancient Halite deposits and/or volcanic xenoliths.
 ●   High-resolution microscopy with automated scanning.
 ●   Advanced etching and plasma cleaning for track enhancement.

Impact:      PRIµS aims to establish the first experimental validation for
paleodetectors, bridging geology and high-energy astrophysics. It could transform the
technique into a powerful tool - not just for cosmic rays, but also for dark matter and
neutrino research - opening doors to studying the deep history of cosmic events. If
the work is successful, we will be setting the stage for breakthroughs and birthing the
field of paleo-astroparticle physics.                                                16

                                        Cosmic rays


From few GeV to hundreds of EeV (1 EeV= 1018 eV)
 ● Most energetic particles known
 ● Produce extensive air showers of secondary
    particles in the atmosphere: in these showers
    are muons, neutrons and neutrinos
 ● Primary cosmic rays mostly charged particles
    and are deflected by magnetic fields: delayed
    wrt to neutral particles
 ● cosmic rays in the TeV-PeV region associated to
    supernova remnants, higher energy may be
    accelerated by compact objects (magnetars?),
    AGN or starburst galaxies
