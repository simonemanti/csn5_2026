The MSPC concept

   THE GOAL:
    ● Develop a new gaseous particle detector
        for mid rate applications: O(200 kHz/cm2)
    ● Space resolution down to 100 μm
    ● Time resolution down to 6 ns
    ● Easier construction w.r.t. MWPC
          ○   reduced assembly person-power

   HOW:
    ● Using photolithographic techniques
        from MicroPattern Gaseous Detetors

   WHY:
    ● for all low-mid rate applications where
        MWPC would still be valid but no longer
        feasible due to loss in expertise or high cost
        due to time-consuming procedure

The MSPC - MacroStrip Proportional Counter
A “wireless” MWPC:
  ●   Field (100μm width) and Sense (30μm width) metallic strips
  ●   resistive glass substrate + backplane
  ●   Field-Sense distance: 2mm

Advantages over MWPC:
 ●   easier to build (reduced personpower: photolithographic process)
Advantages over MPGD:
 ●   good spark suppression (similar to MWPC)

                                                 Summary

                    The MSPC concept merges MWPC robustness and simplicity with MPGD
                    industrial photolithographic fabrication, enabling large-area tracking
                    with O(100 μm) spatial resolution, O(10 ns) timing, O(10⁵ Hz/cm²) rate
                    capability and a great reduction in assembly person-power.

                    In the next two years I will lead the simulation, design, construction and
                    test of prototypes, establishing a validated roadmap:
                    from proof-of-concept to real detector.

                    To carry on this project I will exploit consolidated INFN–LNF and CERN
                    infrastructures, helped by a team with proven expertise in MWPC, GEM
                    and μ-RWELL detectors and an industrial partner already proficient in
                    MPGD photolithography.

                    With this grant, MSPC will become a competitive option for HEP and
                    non-HEP applications, providing a cost-effective, industrially scalable
                    alternative to traditional wire chambers.

The CERN-INFN DLC Sputtering Machine (CID)

                    The CID (CERN-INFN-DLC) sputtering machine, a joint project between CERN and INFN, is used for
                    preparing the base material of the detector. The potential of the sputtering machine is:
                       ●    Flexible substrates up to 1.7×0.6m2
                       ●    Rigid substrates up to 0.2×0.6m2
                    In 2023-24, the activity on CID focused on the tuning of the machine on small foils: good results in
                    terms of reproducibility and uniformity.
                    In 2025
                       ●    0.8×0.6m2 foils have been sputtered with DLC or DLC+Cu
                       ●    5x5cm2 glass samples have been sputtered with DLC, Ti+Cu, Al, B4C
