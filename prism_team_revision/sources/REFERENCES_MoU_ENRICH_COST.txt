The Action will be carried out in accordance with the set of COST Implementation Rules approved by the

The main aim and objective of the Action is to build a European network of researchers and stakeholders

different radiation detection physics sub-communities, creating a cross-border, integrated community where
multidisciplinary knowledge, information exchange, and discussion of new ideas and challenges are central.
This will be achieved through the specific objectives detailed in the Technical Annex.




The present MoU enters into force on the date of the approval of the COST Action by the CSO.

OVERVIEW

Summary
The ENRICH (European Network for radiation-detection based Research and Innovation addressing
increasing societal CHallenges) COST Action aims to build a European network of researchers and
stakeholders working on radiation-detection based experiments and applications.

Numerous researchers work with photon detection daily, applying it in astrophysics, nuclear and atomic
physics, material science, data analysis, simulations, detectors, electronics, optics, medicine, cultural
heritage, food safety, fraud detection and others. However, the community remains fragmented, with sub-
communities (e.g., nuclear physics, synchrotron users) often working in silos. This lack of cross-
disciplinary collaboration limits knowledge exchange and the transfer of technologies from large-scale
facilities to smaller labs and industries.

ENRICH seeks to unite these diverse communities, allowing collaboration across sectors and
disciplines to accelerate scientific and technological advances in radiation detection.

Such a collaboration will, first of all, focus on pressing scientific questions related to radiation detection
in research fields like fundamental physics, nuclear and astrophysics, data analysis, and X- and Gamma-
ray spectroscopy or imaging.

In addition, the scientific outcomes of ENRICH's activities will enhance Europe’s industrial
competitiveness by contributing to tackling key societal and technological challenges Europe faces, such
as energy autonomy, food security, faster medical diagnostics, and more. These challenges require,
indeed, advances in analytical sciences, with radiation-based technologies playing a crucial role across
various sectors of science and society.

ENRICH will be grounded on inclusiveness, and special attention will be paid to young researchers,
those with caregiving responsibilities, and participants from ITC countries, through mentoring programs,
training in proposal submissions, and special initiatives.

1.1.     SOUNDNESS OF THE CHALLENGE
1.1.1.    DESCRIPTION OF THE STATE OF THE ART
The ENRICH (European Network for radiation-detection based Research and Innovation addressing
increasing societal CHallenges) COST Action aims to build a European network of researchers and
stakeholders working on radiation-detection based experiments and applications.
Photon detection research is expanding fast, and further progress demands better resolution (energy,
spatial, and time), efficient optics, advanced simulation tools, and multimodality data. Large-scale
infrastructures like X-ray telescopes, free-electron lasers, and 4th generation light sources are being built
or upgraded. On a smaller scale, brighter photon sources are emerging, using innovative technologies
like liquid anodes or new concepts of particle accelerators and colliders, e.g., driven by high-power
lasers. This drives innovation in detection, optics, and data analysis technologies.
Despite the progress, Europe faces uncoordinated activities within siloed sub-communities (e.g.,
imaging, nuclear physics, synchrotron users), leading to minimal and non-efficient cross-interaction. This
hampers knowledge sharing and slows the transfer of technology from large-scale infrastructures to
smaller labs and industries. Detector markets are similarly segmented, divided by domain, region, or
academic/industrial focus.
Overcoming this fragmentation could yield competitive, high-quality solutions.
The scientific and technological advancements produced by the ENRICH COST Action will allow targeting
key societal Grand Challenges Europe is facing. These challenges raise crucial questions, for example:
•     Industrial and energy autonomy: “can we increase battery autonomy, environmental compliance,
      and lifespan?”; “can we make buildings and machines energetically self-sustainable?”.
•     Food security: “is the food I eat safe? High quality? Can I check it myself?”.
•     Healthcare and prevention: “Can we shorten diagnostic times?”, “Can we detect smaller tumors for
      better treatment?”.
•     Risk of pandemics: “Can we reduce research and testing time for new medicines and vaccines?”.
•     Nuclear forensic and monitoring: “Can we fast and efficiently detect dirty bombs or radiation
      hazards at the national borders or in critical places like airports?”
These challenges can indeed only be tackled through multidisciplinary scientific investigations, where
analytical science significantly impacts every study. Analytical science covers imaging, structural, and
chemical analysis. In this context, X-rays (0.1 keV to 100s keV) and Gamma-rays (up to 1 MeV) are
invaluable for non-destructive investigation of matter. Their penetrating power and ability to excite atomic
core levels allow for detecting signatures of their environment, with direct impacts on crucial societal
aspects like tumor detection (X-ray imaging), swift formulation of vaccines (crystallography), food quality,
security, and counterfeiting prevention (XRF for elemental analysis and chemical speciation), and many
others. Overcoming the present technological limits in photon detection and related applications is the
only possible way towards a successful tackling of Grand Societal Challenges.

Within the ENRICH Cost Action, four main investigation strands have been identified and reported below
including, for each one, a non-exhaustive list of critical aspects to face.

Photon Sources and Optics: Synchrotrons and X-ray free-electron lasers (XFELs) are the gold
standard for X-ray measurements, but they are expensive, scarce, and access is highly competitive.
Tabletop photon sources are a common alternative, but their lower X-ray beam quality (e.g., reduced flux
or tunability) can hinder measurements, especially in low-yield applications like liquid sample
investigations. For example, high-resolution measurements of the Kβ transition line of metallic
compounds, which provide insights into the oxidation and spin states of metals, require energy resolution
at the level of a few eV. This can only be achieved using expensive Transition Edge Sensors (TES) or
Metallic Magnetic Calorimeters (MMC) devices or crystal spectrometers. Applications such as food
quality control demand such measurements on liquid samples, where self-absorption dominates, leading
to low signal rates. This necessitates either high-brilliance facilities, extended measurement times in local
labs or a simpler treatment of the samples to significantly improve the sensitivity down to 1ppb for
conventional table-top EDXRF systems.
A major obstacle in photon detection is the current X-ray optics, which are costly and have low efficiency,
especially at small spot sizes/high resolutions and higher energies. This limits the translation of

techniques from large-scale facilities to smaller labs or to crucial locations such as hospitals, industries,

Photon detection: One major challenge in photon detection is the lack of a single device that offers
together high energy (<0.1%), spatial (a few microns) and time resolution (a few ns or below), and high
detection efficiency. Current technology offers energy resolution of a few %, using silicon detectors (SDD)
for X-rays below 20 keV, CdZnTe detectors up to a few hundred keV, and HPGe detectors for higher
energies. Bragg/Laue spectroscopy achieves much better resolution, but suffers from poor geometrical
acceptance, significantly lower than solid-state devices. Micro-calorimeters like TES or MMC offer much
higher resolution with reasonable acceptance but require complex, expensive cryogenic systems,
operating at very low temperatures (mK range), limiting their widespread use.
Cryogenic cooling is also required for other high-performance detectors, significantly raising costs and
imposing constraints on experimental setups, particularly in limited spaces like accelerators.
Energy and spatial resolution tend to be inversely proportional, meaning high spatial resolution comes at
the cost of energy resolution and vice versa. This impacts techniques like Wavelength Dispersive X-ray
Fluorescence (WDXRF), and improving both resolutions would enhance noise reduction, and the
information gathered from a single measurement.

Electronics and Data Acquisition (DAQ): Pulse processing techniques are inefficient at very high
fluxes, where high-throughput detection systems are required. On the opposite site, rare events and low-
brilliance sources face the challenge of single photon counting. To overcome both these limitations, a big
opportunity for novel hardware and software solutions to develop low-noise detectors and advanced
denoising techniques is brought forward by the interaction between physics and Machine Learning (ML).
Achieving cutting-edge photon detection with combined energy, time, and spatial resolutions requires a
collaborative effort across research teams in solid-state device production (including new materials) and
analog and digital electronics development.
Low-noise analog front-end electronics are needed for sub-keV room temperature energy resolution,
complemented by digital electronics for optimal pulse processing and multi-parameter analysis.
Another challenge is the large volume of data produced by high-quality, fast, and high-resolution
measurements. This “data deluge” issue is time-consuming and labor-intensive to process, especially in
imaging applications, where advancements in pixel size and density lead to exponential data growth, and
it should be also attacked at the DAQ level, implementing new and faster boards allowing, thanks to novel
ML algorithms, for a better online data quality and rejection.

Data Analysis, Software and Ray Tracing simulations: The widespread use of Machine Learning (ML)
techniques in data analysis, image reconstruction, and beam-line control presents challenges,
particularly regarding the need for standardizing data analysis methods and ensuring a broader
understanding of the underlying algorithms to prevent biases. However, ML offers faster and more
accurate software solutions to many experimental problems like, for example, PLS regression which is
used to improve quantification of the results.
For example, experiments with optical elements like slits, mirrors, and crystals require precise alignment
and optimization, which can be time-consuming, especially in low-photon-count applications. At beamline
facilities, where time is limited, long optimization periods can hinder successful measurements.
Additionally, photon detection experiments heavily rely on ray-tracing simulations, which predict beam
dimensions, intensity, and energy. Many experiments require input from external simulation tools, such
as MC simulation codes, to provide physical parameters. For example, using a Bragg spectrometer to
measure photons resulting from particle interactions in an accelerator requires a complex MC simulation
to determine the input source parameters.

Addressing the outlined issues will be accelerated only by developing a broader community that
exchanges information and expertise. This Action's multidisciplinary nature will drive progress in
addressing Grand Challenges like energy crises, climate change, and health. By fostering open and
inclusive collaboration across these fields, ENRICH will facilitate cross-disciplinary and international
research, enhancing the effectiveness and cost-efficiency of global scientific projects in line with the
COST Action's vision and spirit.

sub-communities, creating a cross-border, integrated community where multidisciplinary knowledge,

to address the issues identified in Section 1.1.1 by promoting collaboration among existing communities
to achieve significant advancements beyond the current state of the art, as follows:

producers. Innovations in materials, design, or production methods will make optics more affordable and
efficient, broadening the use of X-rays. At the same time, sharing knowledge among researchers and
producers will allow for the realization of more powerful and efficient tabletop photon sources respecting
the security standards.

Data Analysis, Software, and Ray Tracing Simulations: The ENRICH Action will integrate the
raytracing/wave optics simulations community, which currently focuses on beamline applications, into
the broader radiation detection field. The aim is to develop high-quality ray propagation simulation codes
for general applications and assist research groups in applying these codes. ENRICH will also seek to
enhance collaboration between the raytracing/wave optics community and other MC simulation
frameworks like, for example, GEANT and FLUKA, promoting multidisciplinary experiments. The Action
will leverage Machine Learning to standardize analysis techniques for photon detection applications,
addressing challenges like handling large data volumes and improving experimental reliability.
Additionally, ENRICH will aim to enhance alignment and optimization of experimental setups through
encouraging collaborations towards new automatic self-optimization software using Machine Learning
algorithms.

Electronics & DAQ: ENRICH will help develop novel hardware and software for advanced detection
techniques, fostering joint efforts to improve noise suppression, efficiency at high fluxes, and resolution
in energy, space, and time. Collaboration with stakeholders such as electronic board designers will be
crucial, with the aim of improving the implementation of Machine Learning algorithms in the DAQ boards.
ENRICH aims to develop the new generation readout electronics by reducing noise in front-end
electronics and developing new pulse processing techniques. This includes creating fast, low-noise
charge-sensitive preamplifiers and digital pulse processing electronics, which offer benefits such as
custom filters, stability, and multi-parameter analysis. Enhanced collaboration between researchers in
both detectors and electronics development will improve performance and integration.

Simultaneous advancements in these areas will address societal challenges such as, for example:
battery autonomy (by having a better chemical-physical analysis of cathode and anode materials), food
safety (by identifying tiny traces of dangerous metals), early disease detection (by improving spatial, time
and energy resolutions for better imaging quality), faster medical diagnostics (by reducing the necessary
doses and enhancing the precision tissues investigation with more performant photon detection).

Various groups of subjects will benefit from the ENRICH Action:
Scientists, researchers, and PhD students across fields like Physics, Biology, Medicine, Agrifood,
Chemistry, Environmental Science, Computing, Engineering, Life Science, and many others. They will
gain advanced knowledge in photon detection techniques and applications, enhancing their teamwork
and problem-solving skills for scientific and technological challenges.
Stakeholders, including existing networks, academic and research institutes, healthcare providers,
public and civil organizations, and producers of photon detectors, optical components, new materials for
photon detection, and laboratory equipment. They will directly connect with potential end users, discuss
technological solutions, and gather information on critical issues and needs to guide their research
directions.
The European and non-European populations will benefit from technological advancements enabled
by ENRICH, leading to improved techniques for food quality assessment, medical care, prevention, and
faster responses to energy, climate, and health crises.

1.2.1.      APPROACH TO THE CHALLENGE AND PROGRESS BEYOND THE STATE OF THE

The ENRICH Action will build a collaborative and multidisciplinary network of international experts in
radiation detection, aiming to create a broad, interdisciplinary community where many other scientists
will benefit from individual research expertise. The goal is to advance photon detection science,
impacting fields such as particle and nuclear physics, astrophysics, foundational physics, environmental
science, agrifood, medicine, chemistry, cultural heritage and many others.
This expanded community will disseminate and gather scientific knowledge, discuss recent results and
needed improvements, collaborate on experiment design, train young researchers, and attract funding
through application to national and international calls.
ENRICH will partner with leading industries to support experimental work and develop new tools,
software, and components based on research outcomes.

Technological Efforts (TE) ENRICH aims to pursue, to overcome the present state of art in radiation
detection applications, by fostering discussion and the interchange of know-how and new ideas among
the various communities, include:
TE1: Reduce the gap between energy resolution, efficiency, and cost for radiation detectors by exploring
new materials for photon detection, configurations, and readout techniques.
TE2: Enhance Research & Development (R&D) for photon detectors with high energy, space and time
resolution at room or non-cryogenic temperatures.
TE3: Develop more efficient and cost-effective optics through new manufacturing processes and
materials.
TE4: Improve the brilliance and power of lab-based photon sources like X-ray tubes.
TE5: Integrate advanced raytracing and wave optics simulations with MC simulation software to enhance
multidisciplinary experiments.
TE6: Bring innovations in data acquisition and pre-processing hardware and software, including AI-
powered solutions.
TE7: Standardize new Machine Learning data analysis techniques for broader understanding and use.
TE8: Develop Machine Learning-based self-alignment tools for optical components adaptable to various
applications and measurements.

The above-mentioned TE will be crucial to the development of new tools and instrumentations for several
societal applications, like improved medical diagnostic machines.
The ENRICH vision aligns with the COST Action’s emphasis on knowledge sharing. COST support will
be crucial for networking activities, including meetings, training schools, conferences, workshops, and
Short-Term Scientific Missions (STSMs), with a priority on training young researchers.

effort will be engaging active participants from existing networks to join ENRICH, which will facilitate a
comprehensive census of photon detection research teams, identify suitable intervention areas, and
quickly advance ENRICH objectives.
Existing networks will benefit from improved collaboration and knowledge sharing through ENRICH as
well.

2.2.     ADDED VALUE OF NETWORKING IN IMPACT
2.2.1.    SECURING THE CRITICAL MASS, EXPERTISE AND GEOGRAPHICAL BALANCE
          WITHIN THE COST MEMBERS AND BEYOND
The success of the ENRICH COST Action depends on assembling a diverse and balanced network of
experts and institutions across Europe, with a strategy that involves three key elements:

Engaging Leading Experts: ENRICH will identify and invite top researchers in photon radiation from
various fields, including fundamental, atomic and nuclear physics, astrophysics, environmental science,
biology, life sciences, detector development, solid-state physics, imaging, medical physics, raytracing
simulations, agrifood, material science, nuclear forensic, radiation security, cultural heritage, optics,

emerging talents, while mentoring programs and trainings will be organized to provide guidance and
facilitate connections.
Promoting Inclusiveness: ENRICH prioritizes inclusiveness by involving researchers from more than
20 countries with an almost equal gender balance already at the initial stage. Moreover, special attention
will be given to supporting young researchers and women, as well as to those with caregiving
responsibilities through dedicated activities and childcare facilities. Participation in events for researchers
with caregiving duties will be encouraged by establishing a series of dedicated activities like, for example,
offering in-situ childcare facilities.

Ensuring Geographical Balance: To ensure all participating countries are represented, ENRICH will
monitor and adjust activities for fair distribution. Transparent processes and clear criteria will be
established to ensure equal access to funding and resources, as well as to leading roles in the Action
Management and in the Working Groups.

By engaging top experts, promoting inclusiveness, and ensuring geographical balance, ENRICH will
create a dynamic network that drives innovation and addresses key challenges in photon radiation,
benefiting Europe and beyond.

          POTENTIAL INNOVATIONS AND/OR BREAKTHROUGHS)
The ENRICH COST Action is set to make a significant impact across scientific, technological, and
socioeconomic domains. By fostering interdisciplinary collaboration and knowledge sharing, it will drive
innovation and breakthroughs addressing critical challenges. In Fig. 1, a schematic of the main
anticipated impacts and of the measures to maximize impact of the ENRICH Action are highlighted.

More in detail, the anticipated impacts of the ENRICH Action include:
Scientific Impacts: The ENRICH Action will advance fundamental knowledge of photon radiation
phenomena, including interactions with matter at atomic, nuclear and molecular levels. Integrating
expertise from diverse scientific fields will yield novel insights into photon applications in fundamental,
atomic and nuclear physics, astrophysics, environmental science, biology, life sciences, detector
development, solid-state physics, imaging, medical physics, raytracing simulations, agrifood, material
science, nuclear forensic, radiation security, cultural heritage, optics, photon sources, and electronics.
The promotion of standardized experimental techniques will enhance the reliability and reproducibility of
results, and collaborative research will foster the development of new analytical tools and technologies.
Technological Impacts: ENRICH will significantly influence the field of radiation detectors by
developing advanced detectors with improved capabilities and performances at room temperature. This
will enable smaller, cost-effective experimental setups, devices and tools. Innovations in low-noise
electronics and advanced pulse processing will facilitate room temperature measurements under high
flux conditions. The development of cutting-edge detection technologies will enable, for example, early
detection of diseases and reduce exposure times in medical exams. Additionally, industrial applications
will benefit from enhanced radiation detection technologies, optimizing processes like non-destructive
testing and material analysis crucial, for example, in many cultural heritage or food control applications.
Socioeconomic Impacts: Economic growth and job creation will stem from the development of new
photon radiation technologies, creating market opportunities in high-tech sectors. New analytical
techniques and Machine Learning software will aid in identifying counterfeit products, preventing
economic losses. The commercialization of research findings will generate high-skilled jobs in research,
development, and manufacturing, while SME will be motivated in producing photon detection systems
based on the new developed technologies. For example, advances in medical imaging and radiation
therapy will improve diagnosis and patient care, while enhanced environmental monitoring will safeguard

communities from radiation hazards. Training programs and workshops will elevate skills and expertise,
especially among early-career scientists and those from ITCs, raising the overall quality of research and
education.
Potential Innovations and Breakthroughs: ENRICH will lead to the development of novel machines,
tools and devices for several societal applications, like for example new diagnostic and therapeutic tools
for early disease detection and effective treatments. It will also drive the discovery of new materials with
unique properties, enhancing radiation detection devices and providing new energy solutions, such as
longer-lasting batteries. The expected increased analytical capabilities of benchtop XRF systems or
handheld units will allow non-invasive analysis of samples in perfect agreement with the Green Analytical

economic growth.

3.2.     MEASURES TO MAXIMISE IMPACT
3.2.1.    KNOWLEDGE   CREATION,              TRANSFER         OF     KNOWLEDGE          AND     CAREER
          DEVELOPMENT
The ENRICH COST Action aims to foster knowledge creation, facilitate knowledge transfer, and promote
career development among researchers. By building a collaborative and inclusive network, the Action
will enhance scientific expertise, disseminate cutting-edge research findings, and support member
professional growth.

The strategies to achieve these goals are outlined below.
Knowledge Creation: The Action will support collaborative research projects across diverse disciplines
and institutions. These projects will address key scientific and technological challenges in photon
radiation research, leading to innovative solutions. By integrating perspectives from detectors and
electronics development, astrophysics, atomic, nuclear and hadronic physics, medical imaging, material
science, and other fields, ENRICH will promote interdisciplinary research that advances the state of the
art. Access to research infrastructure will be optimized; the ENRICH network will facilitate access to
shared facilities (like synchrotrons), radiation detectors, and computational tools, enabling high-quality
experiments and analyses. Centralized data repositories will enhance the reproducibility and impact of
scientific findings through efficient data sharing.
Transfer of Knowledge: The Action will prioritize disseminating research results through high-impact
and open access publications, conference presentations, and journal special issues to reach the broader
scientific community. Regular workshops, seminars, and webinars will share research findings, best
practices, and technological innovations.
Knowledge Exchange Platforms, such as websites and online collections, will facilitate information
sharing. Collaborative tools like virtual workspaces will enable real-time interaction among network
members. Outreach to stakeholders will be ensured through joint projects, technology transfer
agreements, and innovation hubs, translating research into practical applications. Developing policy
briefs and engaging with regulatory bodies will help shape policies that support the safe use of radiation
detection technologies.
Career Development: Training will be central to ENRICH. The Action will offer workshops, summer
schools, and short courses to enhance researchers' skills, particularly those from Inclusiveness Target
Countries (ITCs). Mentorship schemes will pair early-career researchers with experienced mentors for
guidance and development.
Mobility and Exchange Opportunities will be crucial. Short-term research visits and secondments will
provide experience in different laboratories and access to specialized equipment. Exchange programs
will foster cross-institutional collaboration and cultural exchange. Career Advancement Support will
include workshops on grant writing, project management, and career planning, alongside recognition
and award programs to motivate researchers.
Attract fundings: Ensuring the necessary financial resources for the various R&D activities will play a
central role in ENRICH. The Action will establish a board of experts to help researchers in successfully
writing scientific proposals by offering workshops, courses and mentoring sessions. This will allow to
attract fundings for the R&D activities and will also serve as career boosting opportunity for young
researchers.
By building a dynamic and inclusive network, the Action will advance scientific expertise, promote
research application, and support member growth, ensuring Europe remains at the forefront of
innovation in photon radiation research.

The ENRICH COST Action is dedicated to ensuring that its findings and innovations are widely

strategies, combined with active dialogue, are crucial.

impact publications in peer-reviewed open-access journals will be a primary method for sharing research
findings. Special issues and thematic collections will highlight the Action’s contributions to the field.
Organizing and participating in international conferences, symposia, and workshops will facilitate idea
exchange and showcase the Action’s research outcomes. Making research outputs, including datasets
and software, available in open-access repositories will enhance visibility and accessibility.

support the direct application of new technologies and innovations in commercial settings. Establishing
innovation hubs or incubators will aid in developing and scaling new technologies emerging from the
Action’s research. Establishing advisory panels with representation from regulatory bodies will provide
ongoing dialogue and collaboration on policy-related issues.
Dialogue with the General Public and Policymakers: Special emphasis will be placed on engaging
the General Public. Organizing public lectures, science cafés, open days, and community events will
engage the public in discussions about photon radiation, its applications, and its societal impact. Through
social media, press releases, and educational materials for schools, such as brochures, videos, and online
resources, the ENRICH COST Action will provide accessible information about the research and its
benefits. Leveraging traditional and social media platforms will help disseminate research findings and
raise public awareness about the Action’s activities and achievements.

and public events to provide hands-on learning experiences related to photon radiation. ENRICH will try

accessible to a broad audience, including students, educators, and the general public.

By engaging with the scientific community, industry stakeholders, policymakers, and the general public,

will enhance the visibility, accessibility, and application of research outcomes, fostering a collaborative
and informed environment that supports the advancement of research on photon detection and its
beneficial applications.

The timeline of the ENRICH Action spans four years, with quarterly checkpoints for tasks and
deliverables. The project begins with foundational activities, such as collections’ creation and platforms’

mid-term reviews ensure continuous monitoring and adjustments. The timeline is structured to support

4.1.3.    RISK ANALYSIS AND CONTINGENCY PLANS
The following is a list of possible risks. For each one, a mitigation plan and the risk level are reported.

Resource Allocation Conflicts: Overlapping tasks and milestones across WGs could lead to resource
constraints. Mitigation: Implement a resource management plan to ensure adequate allocation and avoid
overburdening team members. Risk Level: Low.
Technical Challenges: Unforeseen technical difficulties in photon detection and data analysis could
delay progress. Mitigation: Establish a technical advisory board to provide expert guidance and promptly
troubleshoot issues. Risk Level: Medium.
Collaboration Issues: Lack of effective communication and coordination among diverse WG members.
Mitigation: Schedule regular inter-WG meetings and use collaboration tools to ensure smooth
communication and issue resolution. Risk Level: Medium
Lack of fundings: Lack of financial resources to realize the R&D activities resulting from the networking
activities of ENRICH. Mitigation: In addition to all the activities foreseen by ENRICH for attract fundings,
a resource (tools, instrumentations, laboratories, expertise, etc) sharing plan will be developed. Risk
Level: Medium.
Impact of External Factors: External factors like political instability or global pandemics could disrupt
project timelines. Mitigation: Develop a flexible project plan that can adapt to external changes and
include remote work options, as well as preparing the ground for an efficient eventual organization of
online meetings and events. Risk Level: Low

By identifying these risks and implementing the mitigation strategies, the COST Action can proceed with
greater confidence in achieving its objectives on schedule.
